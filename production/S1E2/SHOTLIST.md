# S1E2 "1,400 Doses" — Clip Shot List

Source: sitcom_final_s1.md (repo @ f1260543), Episode 2, lines 252-355. Dialogue verbatim. 14 clips x ~10s (Gemini video) ≈ 2.3 min + local title card.

## E2-01 — TEASER (visual)

Wide shot, night. Bob carefully rearranges a spice rack where every glass jar is empty, aligning them like a ritual under one warm lamp. Lum watches from the doorway. No dialogue, only room tone and faint psytrance.

Dialogue: (none)

## E2-02 — TEASER (dialogue)

Two-shot at the spice rack. Lum delivers the accusation deadpan; Bob, still aligning jars, corrects him with total sincerity.

Dialogue: LUM: You're organizing your crimes.
BOB: I'm organizing my past. There's a difference. The crimes were just the filing system.

## E2-03 — TEASER (dialogue)

Lum lets the number hang in the air. Bob shrugs without looking up. Lum presses, incredulous and dry.

Dialogue: LUM: One thousand four hundred.
BOB: Give or take.
LUM: Give or take what? A hundred? A felony?

## E2-04 — ACT ONE (dialogue)

Bob makes an impassioned case with hand gestures, jars lined up beside him. Lum listens with deadpan skepticism and sums it up.

Dialogue: BOB: They were free samples. I ordered research chemicals and the Triad threw in an extra 1,400 doses for no reason. Somebody else's decision.
LUM: So you were drowning in it.

## E2-05 — ACT ONE (dialogue)

Bob is matter-of-fact, almost haunted. Lum repeats the number back, genuinely stunned for once.

Dialogue: BOB: I was buried. 1,400 hits I never even asked for. I ate half of them and threw the other half away.
LUM: You threw away seven hundred doses?

## E2-06 — ACT ONE (dialogue)

Close on Bob holding an empty jar, quieter now. The joke drains out of his face as he says it.

Dialogue: BOB: ...And I don't even remember most of the half I ate. That's the part that stung.

## E2-07 — ACT ONE (dialogue)

Bob holds up two identical empty jars, puts them in a line, and delivers the thesis like a lecture. Lum listens despite himself.

Dialogue: BOB: See, this is the thing nobody tells you. Jail is stupid — but at least they're honest about owning you. Out here? They own you and they call it a subscription.

## E2-08 — ACT TWO (dialogue)

Later, darker, one lamp. Bob has stopped pretending to organize. Close-up, exhausted irony, red error screen soft in the background.

Dialogue: BOB: Inside, they say: here's your bed, here's your time, here's your body and we own it.

## E2-09 — ACT TWO (dialogue)

Bob builds a rolling rant, counting freedoms on his fingers. Lum cuts in perfectly flat. Bob points at him: exactly.

Dialogue: BOB: Out here — they say: you're free! You're free to check the dashboard, free to answer the phone, free to pay the thing, free to fix the thing, free to—
LUM: Free to reorder yogurt at four in the morning.
BOB: Exactly.

## E2-10 — ACT TWO (dialogue)

The rant lands soft. Lum delivers the honey bun line like a eulogy. Beat. Bob calls it tragic; Lum corrects him, serene.

Dialogue: BOB: At least the county gives you a honey bun and doesn't ask for a review.
LUM: The county honey bun is the only honest relationship I've ever had.
BOB: ...That's tragic.
LUM: That's accurate.

## E2-11 — ACT THREE (dialogue)

Bob, quiet and direct, no theatrics. Lum answers simply, giving him the floor.

Dialogue: BOB: You want to know the real crime? Not the 1,400. The real crime is the world built a jail that doesn't have walls and then blames you for not noticing.
LUM: And you noticed.

## E2-12 — ACT THREE (dialogue)

Close two-shot. Bob says it plainly, a confession more than a joke. Lum holds his gaze.

Dialogue: BOB: I noticed at seventeen. And I got honest about it. And the honesty looked like a rap sheet.

## E2-13 — ACT THREE (dialogue)

Long pause. Lum picks up one of the empty jars and turns it in the lamplight. Bob corrects him gently.

Dialogue: LUM: You kept the jars.
BOB: I kept the labels. The jars are just... receipts.

## E2-14 — ACT THREE (dialogue)

Bob lines up the last jar. The rack is complete and holds nothing, gleaming under the lamp. The red dashboard blinks behind them. Final exchange delivered like a ritual. End of episode.

Dialogue: BOB: Perfect. Now it's honest.
LUM: Now it's empty.
BOB: Same thing.

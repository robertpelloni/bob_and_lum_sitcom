*Based on 247 transcript sessions (2025-02-25 .. 2026-10-05). Rewritten from scratch 2026-10-13.*

*Two men. One apartment. Three dead friends. A dashboard that has never worked. This is where the bodies are buried — under the pizza boxes.*

## CHARACTER SHEETS

**BOB (Robert Pelloni):** Detroit accent leaking through every sentence. Genius and golden retriever in one body — sometimes in the same minute. Hoodie, always. Builds AI companies at 4 AM. Ate yogurt out of the container while explaining the meaning of God. **Lives on yogurt, coffee, and pizza** — sometimes holds a slice of stale pizza like a cigarette. 15,000 hours on a C++ game engine. Lived in his car. Carried USB flash drives to the library for music — the whole distribution fit in his pocket. Received 1,400 extra doses once — ate half of them, threw half away. Worked five months at Dollar Tree at $13.75/hr so Shonna could pay her credit cards. His father Gregory restored a 1964 Corvette Sting Ray from a barn and never talked about Papu's business. Bob keeps a *list* of the dead. He has never once been right about why they died, and he has never once stopped counting.

**LUM (Lumbros Korlous):** Dry. Deadpan. Occasionally cracks into warmth and it ruins everyone nearby. County jail veteran — honey buns, suboxone, solitary, "I draw. Even when the world is ending." Owns the flip-phone ancestry with Harry — gas-station burner phones, Nokias, StarTAC flips. (Bob's music ritual was carrying USB flash drives to the library.) He was *there* the night Noah died. He has never made it about himself, which is how you know it destroyed him. Creative left brain. He is the one who said *"we are the perfect balanced brain."*

**CHRIS PAPA:** Dead. Seven feet tall, or remembered that way. Angel investor with a 5,000% ROI delusion and a different colored blazer every scene. Carried a big old bag of shrooms and a bigger bag of heart. Took Bob's side in a fight against *Nintendo* that had absolutely no reason to exist. **"Chris died for us both."** Not for Bob. For us *both*. That is the whole point and Bob still hasn't absorbed it.

**NOAH:** Dead. Died *next to Lum*. On a motorcycle night that nobody in this apartment will describe accurately. Bob has been saying it wrong for fourteen years.

**CRYSTAL:** Childhood orbit. Bob knew her at 13–14. Her alcoholic father — "was such a good man that he died to save me." He died to save Bob *and* Crystal, his daughter — spiritually, where at the end of everything there's only love (love and hate are the same spectrum; anyone would die to save another's soul). A dying man already on the brink, he spent his death as a message — "look what you made me do" — and it *divinely* rehabilitated Bob, pulling a boy from a conservative family that wanted him ignorant and safe into kinship with all humanity. God's plan — Godwin's Law, "God *wins*" (extra funny because Godwin's Law is about Hitler). A separate sacrifice. A separate man. Do not merge the bodies.

**SHONNA:** The bills. The utilities. The reason Bob stood at a Dollar Tree register for five months without telling anyone it was heroic.

**HARRY (Halalambos, son of Christos):** Lum's younger brother (42) and Bob's friend. The membrane. "The membrane that keeps it honest, that keeps it pure." Not a brain. Not a heart. The *interface* between two men who each think they're the whole show. Shares the flip-phone ancestry with Lum — the Nokias, the StarTAC flips, the gas-station burner phones.

**GREGORY:** Bob's father. Air Force. Silence as a love language. Barn-find 1964 Corvette Sting Ray restored to show-winner. Hunting trips where nothing was said and everything was said. Won't talk about Papu's business that was supposed to be Bob's.

**FOOD PROPS:** Yogurt, coffee, and pizza (Bob's staples — grocery-store yogurt, gas-station coffee, greasy boxes, cold slices). Honey buns (Lum's weakness — county jail kind, gas station kind, any kind). **Never Pop-Tarts.** The pizza is a metaphor for their entire lives — questionable, probably bad for them, and somehow always there.

**DRUG USE:** Shrooms with Chris. MDMA in the story. Casual, never glamorized — two men who have made peace with their chemistry.

## EPISODE 1: "YOGURT, COFFEE, AND PIZZA"

*Based on: the apartment itself. Every session. The food is the plot.*

### TEASER

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — 3:47 AM**

*The dashboard is on the monitor. It is broken. It has been broken since before this show existed and it will be broken after the heat death. Bob sits in the blue glow eating yogurt directly from the container with a fork. Not a spoon. A fork.*

*Lum enters holding a coffee mug that says I ♥ DETROIT in letters worn down to I ♥ DET.*

**LUM:**
It's four in the morning.

**BOB:**
It's three forty-seven.

**LUM:**
That's worse. That's the *official* wrong answer.

*Bob holds up the yogurt. It is generic. The label is half torn.*

**BOB:**
This is my dinner.

**LUM:**
That's a *crime*.

**BOB:**
Yogurt, coffee, and pizza. The holy trinity. Breakfast of champions and also dinner of men who have given up on the concept of meals.

### ACT ONE — THE ORDER OF THINGS

**INT. KITCHEN — CONTINUOUS**

*The counter is a museum of pizza boxes — stacked, leaning, forming a small skyline. A single yogurt cup stands among them like a lighthouse.*

**LUM:**
You know there's a system. You eat the pizza first.

**BOB:**
I eat the pizza *last*.

**LUM:**
Then you eat the coffee—

**BOB:**
You don't *eat* the coffee.

**LUM:**
You do. I've watched you. You make it so strong it becomes a food group. You eat the coffee, then the yogurt, then the pizza is the *ending*. The pizza is the *credits*.

*Bob considers this with the gravity of a man being told a religion.*

**BOB:**
...The pizza is the credits.

**LUM:**
The pizza is the credits.

**BOB:**
Write that down.

**LUM:**
I'm not writing that down.

**BOB:**
Then the *show* will write it down.

*The smart speaker lights up.*

**AI VOICE:**
Would you like me to order more yogurt, coffee, and pizza?

**BOB:**
...How does it KNOW?

**LUM:**
Dude. It's always listening.

**BOB:**
It's always *understanding*. That's different. That's *worse*.

### ACT TWO — THE BROKEN DASHBOARD

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — SAME**

*Bob pivots to the monitor. The dashboard: six panels, all red, one playing a psytrance track that has been looping for three weeks.*

**BOB:**
Fix the dashboard and you fix the company. Fix the company and you fix the world.

**LUM:**
Fix the dashboard and it breaks again at noon.

**BOB:**
That's not a bug. That's a *heartbeat*.

*He types. The error changes from RED to DIFFERENT RED.*

**LUM:**
Progress.

**BOB:**
*Meaningful* progress.

*A pizza box falls off the stack. It lands open, one cold slice still inside, like a letter from a past self.*

**LUM:**
That's yours. That's the one from Tuesday.

**BOB:**
It's Thursday.

**LUM:**
So it's from *two* Tuesdays ago.

*Bob picks up the slice. Holds it like a prosecutor's pointer.*

**BOB:**
This is my inheritance. Not the Corvette. Not Papu's business. *This*. Cold pizza. A dashboard that won't compile. And you.

**LUM:**
...I'm your inheritance?

**BOB:**
You're the *dashboard*, Lum. You're the thing that's always broken and always here.

### ACT THREE — THE CREDITS

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — LATER**

*4:22 AM. Both men on the couch. Two coffee mugs. One yogurt container, empty. One pizza box, open. The dashboard is still broken.*

**LUM:**
You ever think about how this is the whole show?

**BOB:**
What show.

**LUM:**
*This* one. The two of us. The food. The broken thing on the screen. The psytrance at four in the morning. It's a *format*.

**BOB:**
It's our *lives*.

**LUM:**
Same thing. It was always the same thing.

*Beat. Bob nods slowly. Then faster. Then he's up, pacing.*

**BOB:**
Yogurt, coffee, and pizza. Open on the food. Close on the dashboard. Everything in between is just... the two of us talking.

**LUM:**
That's the show.

**BOB:**
That's the *whole* show.

*The dashboard blinks. Still broken. The psytrance plays. Somewhere a smart speaker adds yogurt to a cart.*

**LUM:**
Don't order the pizza yet. Eat the yogurt first.

**BOB:**
The pizza is the credits.

**LUM:**
The pizza is the credits.

*HOLD on the broken dashboard. The two men. The cold slice.*

## EPISODE 2: "1,400 DOSES"

*Based on: a windfall of 1,400 extra doses — ate half, threw half away — and the honesty of incarceration.*

### TEASER

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — NIGHT**

*Bob is rearranging a spice rack. Every jar is empty. Every jar used to hold something that was not spice.*

**LUM:**
You're organizing your crimes.

**BOB:**
I'm organizing my *past*. There's a difference. The crimes were just the filing system.

**LUM:**
One thousand four hundred.

**BOB:**
Give or take.

**LUM:**
Give or take *what*? A hundred? A felony?

### ACT ONE — THE FREE SAMPLES

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — CONTINUOUS**

**BOB:**
They were free samples. I ordered research chemicals and the Triad threw in an extra 1,400 doses for *no reason*. Somebody else's decision.

**LUM:**
So you were *drowning* in it.

**BOB:**
I was *buried*. 1,400 hits I never even asked for. I ate half of them and threw the other half away.

**LUM:**
You *threw away* seven hundred doses?

**BOB:**
...And I don't even remember most of the half I ate. That's the part that stung.

*Bob holds up two identical empty jars. Puts them in a line.*

**BOB:**
See, this is the thing nobody tells you. Jail is stupid — but at least they're honest about owning you. Out here? They own you and they call it a *subscription*.

### ACT TWO — THE HONESTY

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — LATER**

*Dark now. One lamp. Bob has stopped pretending to organize.*

**BOB:**
Inside, they say: here's your bed, here's your time, here's your body and we own it. Out *here* — they say: you're free! You're free to check the dashboard, free to answer the phone, free to pay the thing, free to fix the thing, free to—

**LUM:**
Free to reorder yogurt at four in the morning.

**BOB:**
*Exactly*. At least the county gives you a honey bun and doesn't ask for a *review*.

**LUM:**
The county honey bun is the only honest relationship I've ever had.

**BOB:**
...That's tragic.

**LUM:**
That's *accurate*.

### ACT THREE — THE RECEIPT

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — NIGHT**

**BOB:**
You want to know the real crime? Not the 1,400. The real crime is the world built a jail that doesn't have walls and then *blames you for not noticing*.

**LUM:**
And you noticed.

**BOB:**
I noticed at *seventeen*. And I got honest about it. And the honesty looked like a rap sheet.

*Long pause. Lum picks up one of the empty jars. Turns it in the light.*

**LUM:**
You kept the jars.

**BOB:**
I kept the *labels*. The jars are just... receipts.

*He lines up the last jar. The rack is complete. It holds nothing.*

**BOB:**
Perfect. Now it's honest.

**LUM:**
Now it's empty.

**BOB:**
Same thing.

*The dashboard blinks red. The psytrance plays. The empty rack gleams under the lamp.*

## EPISODE 3: "COUNTY HONEY BUNS"

*Based on: Lum's county jail. The drawings. The sweetness that's gone too fast.*

### TEASER

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — NIGHT**

*Lum is drawing. The drawing is bad. The drawing is the most serious thing in the room.*

**BOB:**
What is that.

**LUM:**
It's you.

**BOB:**
That's not me. That's a *threat*.

**LUM:**
That's how I see you. In the county. This is the face I drew when the world was ending.

### ACT ONE — THE SWEETNESS

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — CONTINUOUS**

**LUM:**
County jail. You want to know the currency? Honey buns. Not commissary. Not favors. *Honey buns*. The jail kind. The gas station kind. Any kind. You hold one like it owes you money.

**BOB:**
I thought the currency was suboxone.

**LUM:**
Suboxone is the *economy*. Honey buns are the *culture*. You don't understand the difference and that's why you were never in long enough to learn the language.

**BOB:**
I was in long enough to learn the *food*.

**LUM:**
You were in long enough to learn the *complaint*. That's not the same thing.

*Lum holds up the drawing. In it, Bob's face is assembled from terrible lines.*

**LUM:**
I draw. Even when the world is ending. *Especially* when the world is ending. Because if you stop drawing you start *counting* and if you start counting you start *dying*.

### ACT TWO — SOLITARY

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — LATER**

**BOB:**
Did you ever—

**LUM:**
Yes.

**BOB:**
How long?

**LUM:**
Long enough to learn the shape of the ceiling. Long enough to have an *opinion* about the shape of the ceiling. Long enough to draw the ceiling until the ceiling started drawing *me*.

**BOB:**
...That's the loneliest thing I've ever heard.

**LUM:**
Then you weren't listening. It's not lonely if you *commit*. Lonely is what happens when you keep one foot out. I put both feet in and the room became a *studio*.

*Bob is quiet. Then he sits down next to Lum and looks at the drawing.*

**BOB:**
Do me again. Better.

**LUM:**
No. The bad one is the honest one. The good ones are *propaganda*.

### ACT THREE — THE HONEY BUN

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — NIGHT**

*Bob places a gas station honey buns on the table like an offering.*

**BOB:**
For the culture.

**LUM:**
...Where did you get that.

**BOB:**
Gas station. County kind.

**LUM:**
That's the *wrong* kind.

**BOB:**
You said any kind.

**LUM:**
I said any kind when I was *inside*. Outside the rules *change*. Outside the county honey bun is just a pastry. Inside it's a *constitution*.

*They look at the honey bun. It is just a pastry. It is also everything.*

**LUM:**
Don't tell anyone I told you the rules.

**BOB:**
The show will tell everyone you told me the rules.

**LUM:**
The show can *draw* it. Badly.

*He holds up the bad drawing. Bob's terrible face. The honey buns. The dashboard in the background, broken.*

## EPISODE 4: "THE ADAPTER"

*Based on: "God is using me as an adapter." The Hannibal Lecter government. Corporations versus the people.*

### TEASER

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — NIGHT**

*Bob is assembling a computer from parts. Cables everywhere. He holds up a USB adapter like a holy relic.*

**BOB:**
You know what I am?

**LUM:**
Exhausting.

**BOB:**
I'm an *adapter*.

**LUM:**
You're a *person*.

**BOB:**
God is using me as an adapter. Between the thing that has the power and the thing that needs the power. That's my *job*.

### ACT ONE — THE TRANSLATION

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — CONTINUOUS**

**BOB:**
See, most people plug straight into the wall and they fry. They touch the divine and they *fry*. I don't fry. I *translate*.

**LUM:**
You translate God.

**BOB:**
I translate *everything*. I take the fire and I turn it into a voltage the world can *use*. That's an adapter. That's a *dongle*. That's the most sacred job in the entire supply chain and nobody *hires* for it.

**LUM:**
Because it sounds insane.

**BOB:**
Because it sounds *specific*. The world doesn't want specific. The world wants *platforms*. The world wants you to be a platform that takes a cut. I'm not a platform. I'm a *port*.

*He plugs the adapter in. Something sparks. The dashboard changes from red to slightly different red.*

**LUM:**
Progress.

**BOB:**
*Translation*.

### ACT TWO — THE HANNIBAL LECTER GOVERNMENT

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — LATER**

**BOB:**
And the government? The government is Hannibal Lecter. Polite. *Immaculate*. It asks you about your dreams while it's *plating* you.

**LUM:**
That's a metaphor.

**BOB:**
That's a *municipality*. They serve you the forms and the forms have *taste*. They know what you want before you want it because they *raised* you. They grew the want.

**LUM:**
And the corporations?

**BOB:**
The corporations are the *kitchen*. They do the actual cooking. The government just does the *plating*. And me? I'm the adapter in the *wall*. I'm the thing that stops the whole building from burning down when somebody plugs in something *real*.

**LUM:**
And if you unplug?

**BOB:**
Then everything fries. So I *stay*. That's the job. You don't get to quit being the adapter. You can only *upgrade the standard*.

### ACT THREE — THE PORT

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — NIGHT**

*The half-built computer hums. Bob's adapter glows.*

**LUM:**
So what's the voltage?

**BOB:**
The voltage is *us*. The voltage is the show. The voltage is two men in an apartment with a broken dashboard and a list of dead friends, *refusing to fry*.

**LUM:**
That's not a voltage.

**BOB:**
That's *exactly* a voltage. That's the only voltage that matters. Everything else is just... USB-C versus USB-A. It's *politics*.

*Lum laughs despite himself. Bob plugs in one more cable. The dashboard flickers — and for one frame, works.*

**LUM:**
It worked.

**BOB:**
It *translated*.

**LUM:**
...Do it again.

**BOB:**
It only translates once. That's the whole thing about grace. It's not a *product*. It's a *moment* of compatibility.

*The dashboard goes red again. The psytrance plays. The adapter glows.*

**LUM:**
God's using you as an adapter.

**BOB:**
God's using us *both*. I'm just the *port*. You're the *protocol*.

**LUM:**
...Don't put that on a t-shirt.

**BOB:**
I'm putting it on a *patent*.

## EPISODE 5: "THE STING RAY"

*Based on: Gregory's 1964 Corvette. Barn-find resurrection. Air Force silence. Papu's business.*

### TEASER

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — DAY**

*A photograph is on the table: a 1964 Corvette Sting Ray, maroon, gleaming, impossible. Bob stares at it like it's an accusation.*

**LUM:**
That's your dad's.

**BOB:**
That's my dad's *sermon*. He just never figured out how to say it out loud so he said it in *fiberglass*.

### ACT ONE — THE BARN

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — CONTINUOUS**

**BOB:**
It was in a barn. For *years*. Under a tarp and a lifetime of not talking about it. And one day he just... started. Took it apart. Piece by piece. Every bolt is a *sentence* he never said to me.

**LUM:**
What was the sentence?

**BOB:**
I don't *know*. That's the point. It's a 1964 Sting Ray. It doesn't have a *translation*. It has a *chrome bumper*.

**LUM:**
So he restored a car instead of a relationship.

**BOB:**
He restored a car *and* a relationship. He just did them in the wrong order and the relationship came out looking like a *show-winner*.

*Lum picks up the photo. Looks at the car.*

**LUM:**
It's beautiful.

**BOB:**
It's a *confession*. You can't look at that car and not hear what he wasn't saying.

### ACT TWO — AIR FORCE SILENCE

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — LATER**

**BOB:**
Air Force man. They don't talk. They *maintain*. You want to know how my father says "I love you"? He *torques the bolts*. He checks the *fluids*. He does the thing that keeps the thing from *dying*.

**LUM:**
That's... actually kind of deep.

**BOB:**
Don't tell him. He'll torque the bolts *louder*.

**LUM:**
And Papu's business?

*Bob's face changes. Just for a second. The room gets older.*

**BOB:**
Papu's business was supposed to be mine. He won't talk about that. He'll talk about the *Sting Ray* all day. He'll talk about the *hunting*. He'll talk about the *Air Force*. But Papu's business? That door stays *shut*. That's the one sermon he won't preach.

**LUM:**
So the car is the substitute.

**BOB:**
The car is the *substitute teacher*. It teaches the lesson without ever *grading* you. And that's why it's in the driveway and not the *garage*. The garage is for things you're willing to *finish*.

### ACT THREE — THE DRIVeway

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — NIGHT**

*Bob puts the photo away. Then takes it out again.*

**BOB:**
I don't want the car.

**LUM:**
Yes you do.

**BOB:**
I want the *sentences*. The car is just... the delivery vehicle. And it's a 1964 so the delivery *rattles*.

**LUM:**
You want your dad to say the thing.

**BOB:**
I want my dad to say the thing *and then* give me the car. In that order. Otherwise I'm just... a *guy with a Corvette*. And the world has *plenty* of those.

*Lum takes the photo. Puts it in Bob's hand. Closes Bob's fingers over it.*

**LUM:**
Take the car.

**BOB:**
...Why?

**LUM:**
Because the car is *honest*. It's the only thing he's ever finished. And maybe if you *drive* it, you'll hear the sentences. They're in the *engine*. They're in the way it *idles*.

*Bob looks at the photo. The maroon Sting Ray. The barn-find that became a show-winner.*

**BOB:**
The engine idles like a man who's about to say something.

**LUM:**
*Exactly*.

**BOB:**
...I'll think about it.

**LUM:**
You'll *drive* it.

**BOB:**
I'll *drive* it. And I'll listen. And if he says the thing—"

**LUM:**
He won't.

**BOB:**
—then I'll know the *car* said it. And that'll be enough. That'll be the *sermon*.

*The dashboard is broken. The photo is in Bob's pocket now. Somewhere a Sting Ray idles.*

How? Did he die *just* to save you?

**BOB:**
The situation. What he did for *us*. Because he loved his daughter more than I did.

**LUM:**
And Noah?

**BOB:**
Noah died next to *you*. That's the *preposition*. I've been getting it *wrong*.

**LUM:**
And Chris?

*Bob stops dancing. The drums don't.*

**BOB:**
Chris died for us *both*. The list is three names and three *prepositions* and they don't *match* and that's the *point*. Death is not a *metaphor*. Death is a *grammar* and the grammar is *inconsistent*.

### ACT TWO — THE FREQUENCIES

**EXT. DRUM CIRCLE — LATER**

**LUM:**
He thinks the moon is a tuning fork.

**DRUMMER:**
Isn't it?

**LUM:**
...I'm not having this conversation.

*But Bob is already there. Tuned. The drum circle is a frequency and the frequency is a door.*

**BOB:**
God lives in us. That's what the sacrifice is. Not *dying* — *giving*. Chris gave us the *room*. Noah gave you the *night*. Crystal's dad gave me the *lesson*. And now we're *here* and the drums are *tuning* and the moon is *listening*.

**LUM:**
You want to know what the moon is doing?

**BOB:**
*Yes*.

**LUM:**
The moon is *ignoring* you. The moon has been ignoring humanity for four billion years and it's not going to stop because of a drum circle.

**BOB:**
...That's the most comforting thing anyone's ever said to me.

### ACT THREE — THE SUPERNOVA

**EXT. DRUM CIRCLE — NIGHT**

*The drums crescendo. Bob is dancing again. Then he stops. He takes out the list.*

**BOB:**
I'm going to read it.

**LUM:**
Don't.

**BOB:**
I'm going to read it *aloud*.

**LUM:**
...Okay. Read it.

**BOB:**
Noah. Died next to Lum. Chris. Died for us both. Crystal's dad. Died to save us both. *Three men*. Three prepositions. One *room*. And the room is *full*.

*He folds the list. Puts it away.*

**BOB:**
The full moon drum circle is next Friday.

**LUM:**
I know.

**BOB:**
You're coming.

**LUM:**
I *know*.

**BOB:**
And the track is the only thing keeping the void at bay.

**LUM:**
...Maybe. Or maybe the void is just *loud* and the track is just *louder*.

*Bob nods. Then laughs. Then stops laughing. Then nods again. The drums play. The moon hangs. The supernova is not a star — it's the moment the room gets full and stays full.*

**LUM:**
The season's over.

**BOB:**
The season's *never* over. The dashboard is still broken.

**LUM:**
The dashboard is *always* broken.

**BOB:**
Then we're still *here*.

*HOLD on the drum circle. The moon. The two men. The list in Bob's pocket. The void, kept at bay.*

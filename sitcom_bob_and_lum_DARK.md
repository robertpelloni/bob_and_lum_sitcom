# BOB & LUM — Season 1 (DARK CUT)
## "Nothing Happens and Then Everything Does"
### Complete Screenplay — Sardonic / Dark Comedy
#### Every line grounded in real conversation transcripts

---

> **HISTORICAL CUT (canon applied 2026-10-13).** The authoritative rewrites are `sitcom_final_s1.md` (12 eps) and `sitcom_final_s2.md` (8 eps + 6 bonus), plus `sitcom_rewrite_s2.md` (writers'-room cut). This file keeps its original episode taxonomy for reference. Canon held: Bob eats yogurt/coffee/pizza (never Pop-Tarts); Noah died next to Lum; Chris died for us both; Bob was on shrooms with Chris attacking Nintendo for no reason; Lum said "we are the perfect balanced brain."


**SERIES:** Bob & Lum (Dark Cut)
**FORMAT:** Half-hour single-camera comedy-drama. Think *Fleabag* meets *Uncut Gems* in a Michigan basement.
**TONE:** Sardonic, dark, wildly funny. The humor comes from two guys who've been through hell and treat it like weather.
**SEASON:** 8 episodes

---

## MAIN CAST

| Character | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **BOB** (Robert Pelloni) | 43. Gray hair coming in. Wears whatever his mom dresses him in — at 43. Detroit. Multiple rock bottoms — addiction, a couple days in jail, and one *very* long phone call about someone else's foreclosure. The works. Lived in his car in Silicon Valley running 10 miles every morning like a man fleeing a crime scene. Built an olive oil empire by accident. Built DDR arcades by accident. Approached Nintendo with a game he made in an empty room with the windows covered. Has ADHD and self-isolation as a coping strategy. Talks to God and phones with equal desperation. Eats stale pizzas for dinner and calls it a salad. The most optimistic person you will ever meet who has absolutely no reason to be. |
| **LUM** | Bob's best friend. Korean mother, Greek father. Younger brother, sister Agathi, friend Harry. 44. No gray hair yet. A genius artist in every sense — has been to prison AND jail, does heroin, likes honey buns, doesn't scratch, snorts various drugs with the calm efficiency of a chemist. The kind of person the word "wasted talent" was invented for, which he would find funny if he could stop sketching. Calm. Dry. Processes everything through humor because the alternative is processing it. Has been in county jail. Has been shaken down. Has seen the inside of the system and came out with punchlines. |

### RECURRING ELEMENTS
- The Dashboard (perpetually broken — a metaphor Bob refuses to see)
- Stale pizza (a gas station cry for help)
- Psytrance (the only reliable happiness in the building)
- The Phone (judges them in real time, but only when the screen is on)
- The Mortgage Company (on hold forever, like God)

# EPISODE 1: "The Dashboard Is Broken"

*Based on: "Lum & Bob Get Stoned, Listen To Psy-Trance, Talk About AI." + "BOB AND LUM LISTEN TO PSYTRANCE AND TALK AI LIVE"*

**TEASER**

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — NIGHT**

*The kind of dark that only exists at 2 AM in a rented apartment in Michigan. One monitor throws blue light across a desk that looks like a small landfill. Coffee mugs have colonized every surface. Psytrance plays low — Space Tribe or Ajja, the bass doing the emotional work neither of these men will do.*

*BOB sits hunched over a laptop. His face is lit from below like a campfire horror story. LUM is on the couch, one leg over the armrest, scrolling his phone with the dead-eyed focus of a man avoiding his own life.*

*Click. Click. Click-click-click.*

**BOB:**
The dashboard is broken.

**LUM:**
*(not looking up)*
I know.

**BOB:**
No. *Broken* broken. Not "oh, the button's in the wrong place" broken. I mean the kind of broken where you open the file and the code is just... *gone*. Like it packed a bag and left in the night. Like my credit score.

*Lum doesn't react. He's heard the credit score joke. He's heard all of them.*

**BOB (CONT'D):**
I've got a spinner that's been spinning for fifteen minutes. Fifteen minutes, Lum. Do you know what that means?

**LUM:**
That you should restart it?

**BOB:**
It means the spinner is the only thing in my life that's showing *up*. The spinner has more follow-through than I do.

**LUM:**
*(glancing at the screen)*
...Yeah, that's actually broken broken.

**BOB:**
THANK you.

*TITLE CARD: "BOB & LUM" — white text on black. The letters flicker like a monitor about to die. A psytrance bass drop hits and cuts out. Silence.*

**END TEASER**

---

**ACT ONE**

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — CONTINUOUS**

*Bob is pacing. Three steps one way, three steps back. The apartment is small enough that this is basically walking in place with extra drama.*

**BOB:**
Okay. New plan. I find the bug. I fix it. I deploy it. And then I sleep for twelve hours.

**LUM:**
It's after midnight.

**BOB:**
So the twelve hours is aspirational.

**LUM:**
It's a lie.

**BOB:**
All goals are lies until they're not! That's called *manifesting*, Lum! Look it up!

**LUM:**
I'm not looking anything up. I'm trying to figure out if I can sell a kidney on 4chan.

*Beat.*

**BOB:**
...Can you?

**LUM:**
Probably. But the markup is terrible.

**BOB:**
Everything about this economy is terrible. I got an email today — from the *mortgage company*. You know what it said?

**LUM:**
"Dear valued customer"?

**BOB:**
"Dear valued customer." And then four paragraphs that boil down to: *give us money or we take the house*. But they said it in Helvetica, so I guess it's professional.

**LUM:**
Helvetica is the font of quiet desperation.

**BOB:**
See, that's what I'm talking about! You say stuff like that and I feel *seen*!

**LUM:**
I learned it in county jail. The commissary menu was in Helvetica. Very effective. I bought so many honey buns. *(to Bob)* You would have *loved* it.

*Bob stops pacing.*

**BOB:**
...I forgot you were in county jail.

**LUM:**
Everyone forgets. That's the point of jail. It's not the punishment — it's the *editing*.

**BOB:**
*(sitting down slowly)*
How long were you in?

**LUM:**
A year. Maybe more. Time gets... *loose* in there. Like a tooth you keep wiggling.

**BOB:**
What were you in for?

**LUM:**
*(deadpan)*
I don't want to talk about it.

**BOB:**
Okay.

**LUM:**
*(beat)*
...It was dumb. It was all dumb. The whole thing is dumb. But the honey buns were real, Bob. The honey buns were the realest thing in that place.

*Bob stares at him. Then turns back to the screen.*

**BOB:**
My bug is a missing semicolon.

**LUM:**
That's what's breaking it?

**BOB:**
One character. Out of fifteen thousand lines. One semicolon. And the whole thing collapses like a house of cards in a wind tunnel. Like my savings account. Like every relationship I've ever—

**LUM:**
Bob.

**BOB:**
What?

**LUM:**
Fix the semicolon.

**BOB:**
*(typing)*
Right. Fixing the semicolon. Priorities.

**END ACT ONE**

---

**ACT TWO**

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — LATER**

*The coffee mug count has reached six. The pizza box has been joined by a second, older pizza box. Bob is now so close to the monitor his breath is fogging the screen. Lum is asleep on the couch — or pretending to be, which is the same thing at this hour.*

*Bob's finger traces code. His lips move. His eyes stop.*

**BOB:**
*(whisper)*
No way.

*He leans in. His finger stops on an empty column.*

**BOB (CONT'D):**
*(barely audible)*
...You absolute *bastard*.

**LUM:**
*(from the couch, eyes still closed)*
Did you just curse at a computer?

**BOB:**
I FOUND IT!

*Lum opens one eye.*

**LUM:**
The bug?

**BOB:**
THE BUG!

**LUM:**
What is it?

*Bob turns the screen. Points at a blank space with a shaking finger.*

**BOB:**
Right there. Line 8,447. Column 39.

*Lum squints.*

**LUM:**
There's nothing there.

**BOB:**
EXACTLY! THERE'S SUPPOSED TO BE A SEMICOLON THERE!

*Long pause.*

**LUM:**
...How long did you spend looking for this?

**BOB:**
Don't.

**LUM:**
How many hours?

**BOB:**
I said *don't*.

**LUM:**
How many *slices*?

**BOB:**
*(standing, arms wide)*
FIVE! FIVE SLICES OF STALE PIZZA! THE DINNER OF CHAMPIONS AND THE *FUNERAL* OF DIGNITY!

**LUM:**
*(sitting up)*
I need you to understand something. You stayed up until 3 AM. Drank six cups of coffee. Ate five slices of pizza so stale it could qualify as a *weapon*. And the problem was a *semicolon*.

**BOB:**
A SINGLE. CHARACTER.

**LUM:**
You know what that is? That's God. That's God looking down at you and saying, "I could fix your life, but it's funnier this way."

**BOB:**
*(pointing at him)*
THAT'S THE FUNNIEST THING YOU'VE EVER SAID!

**LUM:**
I learned it in county jail. Jail is a comedy writers' room. Very dark. No Wi-Fi. You learn to be funny or you learn to fight, and I have the upper body strength of a *poet*.

*Bob types. Enter. The loading bar fills. A green checkmark. The dashboard loads — clean, perfect, functional.*

*Bob stares at it. His eyes are wet.*

**BOB:**
*(whispering)*
...It's beautiful.

**LUM:**
It's a website, Bob.

**BOB:**
IT'S A GODDAMN MIRACLE, LUM!

*He throws his arms up. Coffee mug #6 crashes to the floor. The phone wakes up.*

**PHONE NOTIFICATION:**
I heard something break. Should I call someone?

**BOB:**
WHO WOULD YOU CALL?! WHO DO YOU *HAVE*?!

**PHONE NOTIFICATION:**
I have your Amazon order history.

**BOB:**
*(to Lum)*
It's threatening me. The speaker is *threatening* me.

**PHONE NOTIFICATION:**
You have 47 frozen pizzas on Subscribe & Save. From *Amazon*. The algorithm has given up on you.

**LUM:**
*(laughing despite himself)*
Forty-seven!

**BOB:**
THEY'RE MULTIGRAIN!

**LUM:**
They're a *cry for help* and Amazon has *automated* it.

**END ACT TWO**

---

**TAG**

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — MORNING**

*Golden light through cheap blinds. Bob is asleep at the desk, face on the keyboard. The dashboard glows above him, working. Keyboard imprints pattern his cheek.*

*Lum enters with two coffees. Studies Bob. Sets one down.*

**LUM:**
Hey. Wake up.

**BOB:**
*(lifting his head)*
Did I fix it?

**LUM:**
You fixed it. You also typed "JJJJJJJJJJJJ" into the commit message.

**BOB:**
...And?

**LUM:**
And I caught it before it deployed.

**BOB:**
*(collapsing)*
You saved my life.

**LUM:**
I saved your *deploy*. Your life is a bigger project.

*Bob takes the coffee. Stares into it.*

**BOB:**
There's going to be another bug tomorrow.

**LUM:**
Probably.

**BOB:**
And I'm going to stay up all night.

**LUM:**
Obviously.

**BOB:**
And eat another slice of stale pizza.

**LUM:**
I'll buy stock.

*They clink mugs. The phone chimes.*

**PHONE NOTIFICATION:**
Subscribe & Save confirmation. 47 frozen pizzas arriving Thursday. The algorithm is *feeding* you now. Like a *pet*.

*Bob buries his face in his hands. Lum laughs — really laughs — and for a second the apartment doesn't feel like a trap. It feels like a *home*. Which is either stupidity or grace. I'm choosing *grace*.*

**FREEZE FRAME.**

**SMASH CUT TO BLACK.**

**END EPISODE 1**

# EPISODE 2: "1,400 Doses"

*Based on: 20260829_211133.txt + 20260829_213328.txt + transcript1.txt (the Triad drug shipment, the FBI, Dark.fail, "jail is stupid — but at least the government admits it owns you")*

**TEASER**

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — NIGHT**

*Psytrance at full volume. LEDs pulse. Bob is pacing like a prosecutor delivering a closing argument. Lum is on the couch, rolling something, the picture of calm criminality.*

**BOB:**
Okay. So when we're talking about this — the board, the browser, the *whole thing* — and then we got the tripping thing, and the Triad sends you—

**LUM:**
What? No. Well, here we go.

**BOB:**
They threw in an extra 1,400 doses of psychedelics.

**LUM:**
...What?

**BOB:**
When the Triads sent me my drugs one time, they threw in an extra free 1,400 doses of psychedelics.

*Lum puts down what he's holding. Stares.*

**LUM:**
Ain't nobody fucking with the Triads, bro.

**BOB:**
That's some boss-level game! That's *customer service*! That's the kind of loyalty program Amazon *wishes* it had!

**LUM:**
"Welcome to Triad Prime. Your free samples are en route."

**BOB:**
And then the FBI got ahead of them.

**LUM:**
They got busted?

**BOB:**
They got *busted*.

*Beat.*

**LUM:**
...How did you feel about that?

**BOB:**
*(long pause)*
I thought it was kind of funny.

**LUM:**
Yeah?

**BOB:**
I felt bad, though. I don't think anybody... *(waves hand)* ...I think jail is just stupid.

**LUM:**
I agree with you 100%. Jail's stupid.

**BOB:**
That's the thing! They were so cutting-edge that they were ahead of us! And the FBI got to them that way!

**LUM:**
You feel bad for the *drug cartel*?

**BOB:**
I feel bad for *anyone* in a cage, Lum. I was in one for a couple days once. It was enough. It doesn't matter what you did or how long — the cage is the same. The honey buns are the same.

**END TEASER**

**LUM:**
Yeah.

**BOB:**
1,400 times 10.

**LUM:**
...14,000.

**BOB:**
FOURTEEN THOUSAND DOLLARS! In *free samples*! That's not a drug deal, Lum — that's a *stimulus package*!

The Triads are better at economics than the Federal Reserve.

You *know*.

I know things, Bob. Things I can't *un*know.

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — THE DARK WEB HOUR**

*Later. The LEDs are darker now. The music is deeper. Bob is on his phone. Lum is looking over his shoulder.*

**BOB:**
What's the difference between this and Dark.fail?

**LUM:**
Let's look at Dark.fail.

**BOB:**
*(holding up phone)*
Dark.fail was... older, it looks like a web page like a fucking Google search.

**LUM:**

**BOB:**
"And the 4.2-star review says: 'Great product, fast shipping, ruined my life. Would order again.'"

**LUM:**
*(snorting)*
"The atmosphere was terrible. Five stars."

**BOB:**
You know what's messed up?

**LUM:**
What?

**BOB:**
The war on drugs. In general. Let's throw 15% of our population in prison for life. Like it didn't ruin their life. Like for no reason. For *nothing*.

**LUM:**
*(quieter)*
I know.

I *know* you know. That's what I'm saying. You *know*.

**LUM:**
I remember I was locked up one time in a county jail.

**BOB:**
Yeah.

**LUM:**
Maybe I was already doing a year. State time. But I got sent back to the—

**LUM (CONT'D):**
It don't matter. It don't matter. I've had cops do good to me, though. That arrested me as gently as possible.

**BOB:**
"Gently."

**LUM:**
Gently is relative! In jail, "gently" means they didn't break anything!

That's the most depressing five-star review I've ever heard.

**LUM:**
"One star. Got arrested. *But nicely.*"

**ACT TWO**

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — THE PHONE CALL**

*Bob is on the phone. His whole body has shifted — he's in "helping mode." The gaming chair is pushed back. He's standing. Pacing. The phone watches.*

**BOB:**

My name is Bob. I'm calling on behalf of— and it's come to my attention that there's been a monthly mortgage statement and amount due of $2,186.64. Are those math-ing for you? Are we math-ing together?

**BOB (CONT'D):**
So we have the money to pay you, but we have a situation here. She's... we're in the process of getting her help and she's falling behind on her mortgage and we don't want her to lose her house. But she fell behind three months. And we're the professional team helping her.

*Beat. Bob listens. His jaw tightens.*

**BOB (CONT'D):**
What do I gotta do, man, to get this settled? For her? I'm trying to help get you guys *paid*.

*He listens.*

**BOB (CONT'D):**
Can I talk to somebody that's a level up from you? ...Yeah, hook me up, brother. Thanks, G.

*He hangs up. Turns to Lum.*

**BOB (CONT'D):**
They won't talk to me. I'm not on the account.

**LUM:**
Because you're not on the account.

**BOB:**
But I'm *here*! I'm the one *doing the thing*! That should count for something!

**LUM:**
In the eyes of Carrington Mortgage, Shonna is nothing. And by extension, so are we.

**BOB:**
*(sitting down hard)*
...Yeah.

**LUM:**
Welcome to America, where the paperwork is the punishment and the punishment is the point.

**BOB:**
That's too deep for 1 AM.

**LUM:**
It's never too deep. It's only too *early*.

*Bob picks up the phone again. Dials. Puts it on speaker. The hold music starts — smooth jazz, the official soundtrack of institutional indifference.*

**BOB:**
*(to the hold music)*
This call may be monitored or recorded for quality assurance purposes. *(beat)* But whose *quality*? Whose *assurance*?

**LUM:**
They can hear you.

**BOB:**
GOOD.

*The hold music plays. They sit in it. The mortgage. The foreclosure. The machine.*

**BOB (CONT'D):**
*(quietly)*
This is what I want to do. This is what I *want* to do. Help people. Keep people in their houses. Is that wrong? Are they going to lock me up for that?

**LUM:**
They might.

**BOB:**
Yeah. They might.

*The hold music plays on.*

# EPISODE 3: "County Honey Buns"

*Based on: transcript2.txt + transcript4.txt (Lum's county jail story, the deputy, the shakedown, suboxone/Xanax)*

**TEASER**

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — NIGHT**

*The apartment is darker than usual. One lamp. The monitors are off. This is a *serious* conversation, which means the music is louder to compensate. Lum is on the couch. Bob is on the floor, back against the desk. Two beers. Not enough.*

**BOB:**
Tell me about county.

**LUM:**
*(staring at the ceiling)*
Which part?

**BOB:**
The part you never talk about.

**LUM:**
I never talk about any of it.

**BOB:**
That's what I said.

*Long pause. Psytrance hums.*

**LUM:**
I remember I was locked up one time in a county jail.

**BOB:**
Yeah.

**LUM:**
Maybe I was doing a year. But I got sent back to the... the *hole*, I guess. Solitary. Whatever they call it now. "Administrative segregation." Because *that* sounds better.

**BOB:**
How long?

**LUM:**
You don't count. You stop counting. That's how you know it's working.

*TITLE CARD: "BOB & LUM — County Honey Buns" — scratched into a concrete wall.*

**END TEASER**

---

**ACT ONE**

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — CONTINUOUS**

*Lum is talking now. Bob is just listening — really listening — which is rare enough to be notable.*

**LUM:**
So we all got shook down, too.

**BOB:**
Shook down?

**LUM:**
You know. They come in. Flip everything. Take what they want. Call it "contraband." I had to put that suboxone in my... *(trails off)* ...somewhere safe.

**BOB:**
...Did it work?

**LUM:**
I wanted that suboxone *so bad*. But I still grabbed like a Xanax and we got all the tobacco and I just like passed it out. I was like, I don't give a...

*He laughs. It's the wrong kind of laugh.*

**LUM (CONT'D):**
It made so much sense at the time. We really were just like taking a bunch of drugs and fucking around. That's it. We were laughing the whole time.

**BOB:**
In *jail*?

**LUM:**
*Especially* in jail! What else are you gonna do? You're in a box with strangers and bad food and a deputy who thinks he's a *warrior*. You either laugh or you become a statistic.

**BOB:**
The deputy?

**LUM:**
Oh, the deputy found out I wasn't in there for drinking.

**BOB:**
What did he do?

**LUM:**
*(shrugging)*
He was... nicer? Which was worse. Because now he's *looking* at me. Like I'm a *project*. Like he can *fix* me.

**BOB:**
Could he?

**LUM:**
Nobody can fix me, Bob. I'm not *broken*. I'm *decorative*.

*Bob snorts.*

**BOB:**
That's the saddest and funniest thing you've ever said.

**LUM:**
That's my whole *brand*.

---

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — LATER**

*More beers now. The lamp is the only light. The conversation has gone past "funny" and arrived at "true," which is more dangerous.*

**BOB:**
Do you regret it?

**LUM:**
The drugs?

**BOB:**
Everything.

**LUM:**
*(long pause)*
...I regret smoking.

**BOB:**
Smoking?

**LUM:**
I still vape. But I'm glad I quit smoking. That was really bad. That's the one thing I'm *glad* about.

**BOB:**
That's... incredibly specific.

**LUM:**
You asked for regrets. I gave you one. The rest are... *archived*.

**BOB:**
Like your liver.

**LUM:**
Like my *soul*. Filed under "miscellaneous." Do not open without supervision.

*Bob looks at him. Really looks.*

**BOB:**
You know what I think?

**LUM:**
What?

**BOB:**
I think you're the most talented person I've ever met.

**LUM:**
Don't.

**BOB:**
I *mean* it. The art you make. The stuff you *see*. That's not nothing. That's *everything*.

**LUM:**
*(quietly)*
It's everything and it's nothing. That's the problem with talent. It doesn't *fix* anything. It just makes the broken parts look *interesting*.

**BOB:**
Maybe that's enough.

**LUM:**
Maybe.

*Silence. The psytrance fills it.*

**LUM (CONT'D):**
We were just taking a bunch of drugs and laughing the whole time. It made so much sense at the time.

**BOB:**
It always does. That's the trap.

**LUM:**
That's the *joke*.

**END ACT ONE**

---

**ACT TWO**

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — THE SHAKEDOWN STORY**

*Bob is refilling beers. Lum is in a rhythm now — the stories come easier with a few in him.*

**LUM:**
And then the shakedown. The *real* one. Not the routine one where they just flip mattresses and pretend they're looking for a phone.

**BOB:**
What's the real one?

**LUM:**
The real one is when they come in and they *want* something. Not contraband. *Something*. A statement. A name. A favor. And you can see it in their eyes — they've got a quota.

**BOB:**
What did you do?

**LUM:**
I gave them what they wanted. And then I went back to my bunk. And I drew. Because that's what I do. I draw. Even when the world is ending, I draw.

**BOB:**
What did you draw?

**LUM:**
Whatever was in my head. Some of it was beautiful. Some of it was... *loud*.

**BOB:**
I want to see it.

**LUM:**
*(smiling)*
No, you don't.

**BOB:**
I *do*.

**LUM:**
Some of it isn't meant for eyes that aren't mine. It's not art, Bob. It's *evidence*.

*The word hangs in the air.*

**BOB:**
...Evidence of what?

**LUM:**
That I was *here*. That I *survived*. That the honey buns were *real*.

*They both laugh. The laughter is a shield. Both of them know it.*

**END ACT TWO**

---

**TAG**

**INT. BOB'S APARTMENT — LATE NIGHT**

*Empty beers. The lamp is dying. Both men are on the couch now, shoulder to shoulder.*

**BOB:**
Hey Lum?

**LUM:**

### Tonal Principles
- The humor is **sardonic**, not slapstick — these men are funny because they've been through hell and treat it like weather
- The darkness is **real** — addiction, jail, foreclosure, the AI apocalypse — never played for cheap shock
- The laughter comes from **recognition**, not jokes — "that's the most American thing I've ever heard"
- Every emotional beat is **earned** — then immediately undercut with a joke, because that's how these two actually survive
- The phone is the **id** of late capitalism — always listening, always selling, occasionally prophetic
